## The method — Find-Its-Home (repeatable, all measurement)

Each closed signal runs five steps, none of which spends multiplicity: **confront** the old verdict
and its epoch → **characterize** the signal universe-wide (is the information real?) → **diagnose the
book-fit** (why did it die on *this* book?) → **locate the home** (which book shape's phase / horizon
/ turnover expresses it?) → **place it** (record "real, home = shape Y"), never kill. Only if a home
is found *and* it clears the activation bound does a candidate earn a gated trial. The template is
finding **0139** (delivery).

## The pattern that decides the review

**The homes converge on one thing: a second book shape with its own capital.** Delivery wants a
pre-breakout / breadth-50 vehicle; the zoo wants an own-capital breakout sleeve; USD-sensitivity
(A7) wants a portfolio ERC sleeve; low-vol (A6) *is* the first such sleeve, already live-logging. So
the campaign is not 13 unrelated studies — it is the **assembly of the signal set for the second
book** whose go/no-go is **§4 (breadth-50)**. The measurement half is unlimited and free; only the
book-building half is the gated, owner-capital decision everything funnels toward.

And the counter-examples are as valuable as the wins: **not every real signal is placeable.** PEAD is
real and structurally trapped; the zoo's edge is real but its sleeve economics are unfavorable. The
campaign will keep producing a *mix* of "found a home" and "stays closed" — which is the honest
calibration a blanket re-open would have missed.

1. **Low-vol is live** — the first second-sleeve is forward-logging at 0.60; its Oct-1 first read is
   the earliest real evidence on whether the diversification free-lunch holds forward.
2. **Delivery has a named home here** — a pre-breakout / broad book is the vehicle that could express
